# Positioning: what hiring now weights

The writing rules in `AGENTS.md` follow from evidence about what hiring managers assess. The main source is Anthropic's "How employment is changing at Anthropic" (Wilmers, Catipon, Massenkoff and McCrory, October 2026; https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic), which coded hiring-manager interview scorecards for thousands of hires since 2024. It describes one AI-intensive employer and its authors warn that its practices "cannot be replicated across the economy"; the harness treats it as the leading indicator for AI-heavy employers, not as a law.

## What the scorecards shifted toward

- **Demonstrated creative use of AI tools.** "The clearest change": mentions tripled for technical roles and quadrupled for non-technical ones between 2024 and 2026. A resume shows this with what the person built or automated with AI in their own work, with a result, not with a list of model names.
- **Autonomy and judgment** over domain expertise. For technical candidates "the single biggest increases were in comments on applicants' autonomy and judgment. Discussion of domain expertise shrank." A bullet therefore states the decision the person made and what it cost to decide otherwise, before the mechanism.
- **Range across domains.** "I expect models to continue to be jagged, so for my team I want them to be fungible across domains." Breadth is evidence, if each domain shows the same judgment applied, not a list of unrelated tools.
- **Customer and mission orientation** for non-technical roles, while "discussion of execution skills fell."
- **Agent management as the growing work.** "Writing specs for multi-agent runs, looping one agent to review another's output, and monitoring pipelines"; standing instructions for agents grew from a few hundred to 8,500 in a year; employees "spend more time building the tools needed to [manage teams of Claudes] in their own distinctive domains." Tooling a person built to run agents is resume material of the first rank.
- **Working and deciding in the open.** "Private work, in unlogged meetings and direct messages, is invisible, confusing and discouraged in an AI-heavy workplace." Decision records, written rules and visible pipelines are evidence of a habit employers now select for.

## What it shifted away from

Implementation ("writing code, identifying bugs, logging progress, drafting emails") is what the report says was automated, and junior technical hiring fell from 27% to 9% of technical hires. Algorithmic detail, migration steps and tool inventories earn less than they did; keep them where a posting asks for them and cut them elsewhere.

## How the rules follow

- Lead the summary with the scope of judgment the person exercises and where, then the breadth, then credentials.
- Order skills groups so the target's scorecard fills the first lines a recruiter skims.
- Every bullet: decision or action, scope, measured result, each traceable to a fact in `interviews/`.
- Prefer the enterprise name of a practice (change control, demand management, enablement program, release engineering) because it is the word the scorecard and the posting use.
- When a posting exists, its own words win: mine it into the target's **Scorecard** and write to that.
